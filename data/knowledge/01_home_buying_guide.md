# Home Buying Guide: From Budget to Keys

## Step 1: Set a realistic budget
A common rule of thumb is the 28/36 rule: housing costs (mortgage, property tax, insurance) should stay under about 28% of gross monthly income, and total monthly debt payments under about 36%. Lenders use similar ratios, but a lender's maximum is not always a comfortable budget. Remember costs beyond the mortgage: property tax, home insurance, utilities, maintenance and any homeowners association (HOA) fees.

## Step 2: Plan the down payment and closing costs
A down payment of 20% of the price usually avoids private mortgage insurance (PMI) on a conventional loan. Smaller down payments are possible, for example 3% to 5% on some conventional loans and 3.5% on FHA loans for qualifying buyers, but they raise the monthly payment and add insurance. Closing costs commonly run 2% to 5% of the purchase price and cover lender fees, appraisal, title insurance, recording fees and prepaid taxes and insurance. Keep an emergency fund in addition to these amounts.

## Step 3: Get pre-approved
A pre-approval letter shows sellers you are a serious buyer and tells you the loan amount a lender is willing to offer. Compare offers from several lenders: interest rate, fees, loan term (15 or 30 years) and whether the rate is fixed or adjustable. A shorter term has a higher monthly payment but far less total interest.

## Step 4: Search and compare homes
Compare homes on price per square foot, build quality, age, lot size, garage and neighborhood. Price per square foot is useful for comparing similar homes in the same area, but it can mislead across neighborhoods or between very small and very large homes. Look at what similar homes nearby actually sold for, not only the asking price.

## Step 5: Make an offer with protections
An offer states the price, earnest money deposit (often 1% to 3% of the price), closing date and contingencies. Common contingencies let you back out or renegotiate if the inspection finds serious problems, if the appraisal comes in below the price, or if financing falls through.

## Step 6: Inspection and appraisal
A professional home inspection checks the roof, foundation, plumbing, electrical system, heating and cooling, and signs of water damage. Use the report to request repairs or a lower price. The lender orders an appraisal to confirm the home is worth the loan amount. If the appraisal is lower than the price, you can renegotiate, pay the difference in cash, or walk away if you have an appraisal contingency.

## Step 7: Close the deal
Before closing, do a final walkthrough, review the closing disclosure line by line, and confirm the funds needed. At closing you sign the loan documents, the title is transferred and you receive the keys.

## Red flags during a home search
- Signs of water damage, a cracked foundation or an old roof near the end of its life.
- A price far below similar nearby homes without a clear explanation.
- Seller unwilling to allow a professional inspection.
- Unclear ownership, unpaid liens or unpermitted additions.
