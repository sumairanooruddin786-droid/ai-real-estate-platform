# Legal and Paperwork Checklist for Buyers

This checklist is educational. Property law differs by country and region, so confirm every step with a licensed real estate attorney or conveyancer in your area before you sign anything.

## Confirm ownership and title
- Ask for the title deed and verify the seller is the registered owner.
- Run a title search to find liens, unpaid mortgages, unpaid taxes or legal claims against the property.
- Title insurance (where available) protects against ownership disputes found after closing.

## Read the sale agreement carefully
- Names of buyer and seller, exact property address and legal description.
- Price, deposit amount, payment schedule and closing date.
- Contingencies: inspection, appraisal, financing and any repairs the seller agreed to make.
- What happens if either side cancels, including whether the deposit is refunded.

## Check the property's legal status
- Zoning: confirm the property can be used the way you plan (residential, rental, business).
- Building permits: additions and renovations should have approvals. Unpermitted work can force you to pay for fixes later.
- Easements and boundaries: ask for a survey to confirm lot lines and any shared access.
- Homeowners association rules or covenants: fees, rental limits, exterior rules and any special assessments.

## Seller disclosures and inspections
Many regions require sellers to disclose known defects. Review the disclosure, then compare it with your own inspection report. Ask about past flooding, structural repairs, pest problems and environmental hazards.

## Taxes and ongoing costs
- Ask for the last property tax bill and find out how the tax is calculated after a sale.
- Learn about transfer taxes, stamp duty or registration fees that apply at purchase.
- Confirm utility accounts and any unpaid utility bills are settled by the seller.

## Documents to keep
Keep copies of the signed agreement, closing statement, title documents, inspection reports, loan papers, insurance policy and receipts for major repairs. You will need them for taxes, insurance claims and a future sale.
