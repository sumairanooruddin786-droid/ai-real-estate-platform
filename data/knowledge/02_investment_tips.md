# Real Estate Investment Tips

## Know the core numbers
**Net operating income (NOI)** is yearly rent minus operating costs such as taxes, insurance, maintenance, management and vacancy. It excludes mortgage payments.
**Capitalization rate (cap rate)** equals NOI divided by the purchase price. A higher cap rate means more income per dollar invested, usually with more risk or a weaker location.
**Cash-on-cash return** equals yearly pre-tax cash flow divided by the cash you actually invested (down payment, closing costs and repairs). It shows the return on your own money when you use a loan.
**Gross rent multiplier** equals price divided by yearly gross rent. It is a quick way to compare rental properties but ignores expenses.

## Quick screening rules, used with care
The "1% rule" says monthly rent should be about 1% of the purchase price. It is only a fast filter, and many markets fail it. Always replace it with a real calculation of expenses.

## Budget for costs people forget
- Vacancy: plan for some months without a tenant each year.
- Maintenance and repairs: a common planning figure is about 1% of the property value per year, more for older homes.
- Capital expenses such as a new roof, heating system or appliances.
- Property management fees if you do not manage the property yourself.
- Property tax reassessment after purchase and rising insurance costs.

## Location drives long-term value
Neighborhoods with strong employment, good schools, nearby universities or hospitals, and good transport tend to hold value and attract tenants. Study how long homes take to sell and how prices in the area moved over several years, not just the last few months.

## Value-add versus buy-and-hold
Value-add investors buy below-average condition homes and improve them. The biggest price gains in housing data usually come from raising overall build quality, adding usable living space, and improving kitchens and bathrooms. Cosmetic upgrades are cheaper but have smaller effects. Always compare renovation cost with the likely rise in value before you start.

## Manage risk
- Do not stretch your budget to the lender's maximum.
- Keep cash reserves for several months of mortgage and repairs.
- Diversify across locations if you build a portfolio.
- Check that you could still afford the loan if interest rates rise or the home stays empty for a while.
- Real estate is illiquid: selling can take months and costs several percent of the price.
