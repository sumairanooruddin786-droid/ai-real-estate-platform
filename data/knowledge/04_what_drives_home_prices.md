# What Drives Home Prices

Studies of housing sales data, including the Ames, Iowa dataset used in this project, find that a small group of features explains most of the difference in price between homes.

## The biggest factors
1. **Overall build quality and finish.** Higher material and finish quality has one of the strongest effects on price. Moving a home up one quality step raises the typical price noticeably.
2. **Living area.** Above-ground living space is the most important size measure.
3. **Location.** Neighborhood alone separates budget areas from premium ones. Two similar homes in different neighborhoods can sell for very different prices.
4. **Basement and total space.** Finished or large basements add usable space and value.
5. **Garage capacity.** Space for more cars generally adds value.
6. **Age and renovation.** Newer homes usually sell for more. A recent remodel narrows the gap for older homes.
7. **Kitchen quality.** Kitchens are among the most visible rooms; a better kitchen rating is linked with higher prices.
8. **Extras.** Fireplaces, larger lots and extra bathrooms contribute smaller amounts.

## Reading a price estimate
An estimate from a statistical model is a starting point, not an appraisal. It does not see the condition of the roof, the quality of the street, the view or the negotiation. Treat the shown price range as the likely band, and expect real sales to land outside it from time to time.

## Spotting a good deal
Compare the asking price with the estimated value and with the neighborhood median. A price well below the estimate can signal a bargain, or it can signal a problem the data does not capture, such as deferred maintenance. Always inspect before concluding it is a bargain.
